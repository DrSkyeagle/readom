# E-paper interface rules

These apply to every interface this project controls the look of: the framework's default theme (dialogs, popup menus, toasts -- which a third-party application without a theme of its own also gets), the system interface (SystemUI), Settings, and the home screen (Launcher3).

What the PW3 is: a 6-inch Carta e-paper panel, 1072x1448, 300 ppi, 16 grey levels; no GPU, so the system interface is drawn in software; density 1.875 (1 dp = 1.875 px = 0.159 mm).

## In one sentence

**Separate things with lines and space, not with shadow, lightness or colour; keep what changes to pure black and white pixels; and let a large change happen in one step, with no frames in between.**

## The rules

| # | Rule | Value | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | White ground, black text | The ground is the panel's level 15 (`#FFFFFF`), text its level 0 | The panel's white reflects only about 42%, so the ground has to be its brightest level; E Ink's GL16 waveform is made for sparse content on white. A light grey such as `#F5F5F5` or `#EEEEEE` lands on level 14 here, which is all but indistinguishable from white and cannot separate one area from another |
| 2 | Emphasis is black, state is shape | Selection, switches and checkmarks say so with at least two properties: filled against outlined, weight, a thick underline | Material's default teal accent lands on level 2 here -- a dark grey barely lighter than the text. WCAG 1.4.1 says colour may not be the only carrier of information |
| 3 | Anything floating on the page (dialogs, menus, toasts, cards, the search field) gets a black outline instead of a shadow | Pure black, `1dp` (2 px after rounding here); corner radius `4dp` | Software rendering does not draw elevation shadows at all, so a white surface on a white page has no edge. The outline is pure black because a grey one needs 3:1 against white, which means level 5 or darker, and because a 1-2 px line already reads lighter than a block of the same colour. The width is written in whole pixels or an anti-aliased grey edge appears. The radius is a matter of style and no evidence was found for or against any particular value. **A stack of entries on one surface is not boxed one by one**: an outline says "this thing floats above the page", while a column of entries has to say "the one before ends here", so entries are parted by a rule of the same black and the same width rather than by four edges each. The same thing floating is still boxed -- a notification is a rule in the shade and is outlined on all four sides while it floats over the page as a heads-up (frameworks/base 0065 splits the two on `isPinned()`). On 2026-10-28 the reader compared both on the device for the notification shade and chose the rule |
| 4 | Nothing dims behind a dialog | `backgroundDimAmount` is 0 | Dimming by 60% turns a white page into level 2 dark grey: opening and closing each cost a full-screen 16-level waveform (about 463 ms) and leave a ghost. At 0 the window manager does not show the dim layer at all (`DimLayer` stays hidden at alpha 0) |
| 5 | No gradients, no translucency, no animation | The three animation scales default to 0; gradients become a flat colour or go | A gradient shows as bands on 16 grey levels. Every frame here is a panel update (A2 134 ms, DU 216 ms, 16-level 463 ms), so a 200 ms animation puts at most one or two grey half-drawn pictures on the screen |
| 6 | A gesture over a large area takes effect in one step, when the finger lifts | Turning a page, pulling out a panel, opening all applications: drawn once on release. A small continuous control (the brightness slider) may follow the finger, as long as tapping and stepping still work | Past about 25 ms of lag a drag starts to feel worse, and the fastest frame here is 134 ms: following the finger over a large area only produces a string of half-drawn pictures |
| 7 | A touch target's size follows its position | A list row in the middle of the screen >= 48 dp (7.6 mm); a navigation or tool bar against an edge >= 56 dp (8.9 mm); a corner >= 64 dp (10.2 mm), or spaced away from its neighbours; spacing >= 8 dp | Measured studies: the size a finger needs is about 7 mm at the centre of a screen, about 9 mm at an edge and about 12 mm in a corner (R95). A mis-tap costs more on e-paper: the wrong screen has to be drawn (216-463 ms) and drawn again on the way back |
| 8 | Size and weight of type | Interface text that is read continuously >= 16 sp, secondary notes >= 14 sp, and 12 sp only for a short label taken in at a glance, such as a time or a page number; small type only in Regular and Medium | From the critical-size studies (Latin x-height 12', Chinese about 17% larger), converted for the 35-40 cm this machine is read at |
| 9 | Large areas of pure black belong to things that stay put | A large dark area that appears and disappears often (a whole row inverted, a dark panel) is not used | A large dark area ghosts worse when it appears and disappears |

Rules 3, 4 and 5 rest on the most. The particular numbers (2 px, 4 dp, 56 dp) are starting points with a reason behind them; they have not yet been compared against alternatives on this machine, and what is missing is listed under "Known limitations" below. No study was found that compares outline weights, corner radii or touch sizes on e-paper specifically. The basis is a study of 38 sources (reading research, display standards, measured interaction studies); the study itself is not published with the repository.

## Where the numbers are defined

Three of them: `pw3_outline` black, `pw3_outline_width` 1dp, `pw3_corner_radius` 4dp. This document is the authority, and all of the places below change together.

| Scope | Where | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The framework's dialogs, popup menus and toasts | frameworks/base 0023: written out in `dialog_background_material.xml`, `popup_background_material.xml` and `toast_frame.xml`; 0054 writes out the line along the bottom of the immersive-mode cling in its layout | See the explanation below |
| SystemUI | frameworks/base 0024: `packages/SystemUI/res/values/pw3_eink.xml` | The recents cards, the "clear all" button, the power menu (0029) and the rule between notifications (0059, 0065) |
| Settings | Settings 0004: `res/values/pw3_eink.xml` | The search bar, the suggestion cards and the rule under a title bar (0008, 0009) |
| The package installer (PackageInstaller) | PackageInstaller 0004: `res/values/pw3_eink.xml` | The rule under each screen's header band (0004, 0005); colour and width only, no radius |
| Home screen | Launcher3 0007: `res/values/pw3_eink.xml` | The search field; the page outlines of edit mode (0008, which adds a doubled `pw3_outline_width_double`) |
| The WeRead downloader | written out in `device/amazon/pw3/apps/WeReadInstaller/res/drawable/outlined_button.xml` | Its button |

**The inset of a rule is written in whole pixels.** The rule under a title bar is a layer-list: black underneath, with the ground on top stopping one line width short of the bottom. An inset is read with `getDimensionPixelOffset()`, which truncates, so 1dp x 1.875 gives 1 px; an outline (`<stroke>`) is read with `getDimensionPixelSize()`, which rounds, and gives 2 px. So Settings and PackageInstaller carry one more value, `pw3_rule_inset`, written as `2px` (this panel has one density).

**Why the framework gets no single shared resource**: adding a resource name to framework-res changes the resource IDs (aapt2 assigns them in name order), which changes framework.jar and the boot image. A changed boot image means every application's machine code in `/data` is compiled again, and until it is, an application such as WeRead falls back to the interpreter (measured during development: opening a book then took 53.7 s). So the framework only changes the values of resources that already exist, and each application carries its own copy of the same numbers. Launcher3 is compiled against the public SDK and could not refer to a private framework resource in any case.

For the same reason, **when overriding or changing a framework resource, the comment directly above the entry is kept word for word from AOSP**, and the PW3 explanation goes in the file header outside `<resources>`. aapt2 copies the comment immediately above an entry into `com.android.internal.R` as Javadoc; change the number of comment lines and the line numbers inside framework.jar change, and the boot image with them. Overriding the navigation bar height was tried during development: even with the comment copied from AOSP, the generated R.java had one line fewer at that entry and the boot image changed, so the navigation bar height was left alone. The inferred cause is that the resource has a second commented definition in `values-sw900dp`, and after the override that comment no longer appeared in R.java; aapt2's merge was not verified line by line.

**That a comment changes the boot image has been measured.** Patch 0060 changes one value in `core/res/res/values/colors.xml`, `notification_action_list`, and its first version carried seven lines of PW3 explanation directly above the entry. What is compared is `boot_image.sha256` in the export's `build-result.json` (the hash of the 45 boot image files; the payload manifest carries the same number as `boot_image_sha256`, which is what the installer reads to decide whether applications are compiled again after an upgrade):

| build | `boot_image.sha256` |
|---|---|
| The seven builds before 0060 | `73db7fc9d00b36b16b0295034c2c0f49eccf92683a33ee4058c0c6d92123c3d4` |
| 0060's first version, seven comment lines above the entry | `ba857c7b91108dd45674ae86ca31eac65f6127a56a3abff4bb5ec1672086ccfc` |
| With the comment moved into the patch header (three rebuilds) | `73db7fc9…`, the same as before |

The value itself never reaches the boot image (values live in resources.arsc); what moved was the line numbers of the classes declared below the comment in the generated `R.java`, and those line numbers are in framework.jar. So there is exactly one thing to do before a framework resource change ships: compare that field after the build.

## The interfaces brought in line

| Interface | Change | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogs, popup menus, toasts | White ground, black outline, 4dp radius; no dimming (Material, Holo and the base themes) | frameworks/base 0023 |
| The light theme | The ground goes from grey 50 to white and the accent from teal to black (switches, checkmarks, progress bars, sliders, dialog button text and the caret follow) | frameworks/base 0023 |
| Recents | One screen of 2 x 3 icon cards, newest at the top left; a white title bar with black text; outlines on the cards and on the "clear all" button | frameworks/base 0024 |
| Quick settings | The gradient scrim above the navigation bar is gone | Device overlay `SystemUI/res/drawable/qs_navbar_scrim.xml` |
| The navigation bar | AOSP's own three buttons -- back, home, recents -- are kept; they do not fade once the finger has left them | The buttons are AOSP's; not fading is frameworks/base 0022 |
| The home screen | Swiping up opens all applications and swiping down at the top of the list closes it, each in one step on release; no arrow hint, no bounce, no gradient on the page | Launcher3 0006 |
| "All applications" on the home screen | One cell on the home screen, with the same icon and label as the three applications beside it; one tap opens the list in place and starts no activity; a long press does not pick it up and offers no removal, and dragging another icon onto it makes no folder | Launcher3 0014 |
| The five home screen marks | One existing family (Material Symbols Rounded, filled; a 960-unit canvas, one weight, no strokes): all applications, files, settings, browser, WeChat Reading. Each is knocked out of a black rounded tile, which is the shape every application icon on this screen already has; a panel without colour recognises a mark by its mass, and flat black and flat white are the two ends it renders most cleanly. The launcher used to put a white tile and a shadow behind an application's icon and nothing behind an old-style shortcut, so the two were never consistent; both are turned off | Launcher3 0017, `device/amazon/pw3/launcher-icons/` |
| The size of the home screen marks | **A 112 px canvas, a 90 px tile, a 51 px mark, a 20 px radius.** 112 is this screen's own `launcher:iconSize 60dp` x 1.875. 90 is not taste: the launcher shrinks an **application's** icon through `IconNormalizer` and leaves its own drawables alone, so one family came out in two sizes (measured during development: 93 px against 113 px). Working `IconNormalizer`'s formula backwards, the largest tile it leaves alone is 92.5 px, and 90 keeps the anti-aliased pixel in hand -- the drawing then already satisfies its test, the scaling step does nothing, and all five reach the screen at the size they were drawn (all five measured 91 px) | The derivation in `launcher-icons/make_icons.py`, and `tools/tests/test_icon_normalizer.py` |
| The first cell on the home screen | Without WeRead installed it is the downloader's cell (its mark carries a download arrow); once the install succeeds **the downloader hands the cell to WeRead** by updating its own row in the home screen table, so the replacement happens wherever the cell has been moved to, and the downloader then takes itself off | The downloader's `HomeScreenCell` (nothing on the system side mentions WeRead at all) |
| The home screen's search field | White ground, black outline | Launcher3 0007 |
| Settings | The search bar and the suggestion cards: white, outlined, no shadow | Settings 0004 |

A few base changes besides: animation scales of 0 by default (frameworks/base 0002 and the device overlay `SettingsProvider/res/values/defaults.xml`), the gradients out of SystemUI (0013), opaque white system bars with black icons (0015 and the overlay), and a caret that does not blink (0016).

## What this costs, and what it saves

| Change | Effect | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| The framework only changes resource values | The boot image does not change, so the applications' machine code is still valid | Checked during development: before and after a build that only changed resource values, the 45 boot image files were byte-identical |
| No dimming | Faster: a full-screen 16-level refresh saved on each opening and closing of a dialog, and no CPU dim composition either | `DimLayerController` does not call `show()` when dimAmount is 0 |
| Outlines | Negligible: one more rectangle path per floating surface | Software Skia, drawn once when the surface appears |
| Six recents cards | At most three more icons read on the first opening (it used to show three cards' title-bar icons); nothing scrolls, so looking through tasks no longer triggers a panel refresh | There are still no task thumbnails, and the icons come from the task loader's existing cache |
| Swiping up on the home screen | Less: nothing is redrawn during the swipe and the screen refreshes once on release | The animation scale is 0, so the transition ends on its first frame |

The effects above are worked out from the code. How long Settings and recents take to open, and which areas and waveforms the kernel log shows when a dialog opens and when the home screen is swiped, have not yet been measured on the device.

## Known limitations

- Outline width and divider grey have not been compared on this machine: 2 px or 3 px (with 3 px looked at separately for top-level windows such as dialogs), and which grey level a divider gets; what is missing is photographs of one test chart on the panel.
- Ghosting after a black-outlined surface is opened and closed repeatedly has not been measured on its own. When a window appears or goes away, the display service already redraws the area it covered with one GC16 (`replaced()` in `device/amazon/pw3/epdc/Panel.h`), at the cost of that area flashing once.
- Press feedback has not been measured: whether the framework's ripple is visible at all with animation at 0, and whether a black inner border on the button would be quicker; what is missing is slow-motion video (240 fps) set against the kernel log.
- Rule 7's three touch sizes come from the literature; this machine's touch accuracy has not been measured. The navigation bar is still AOSP's 48 dp rather than the 56 dp rule 7 asks for; changing it means overriding the framework resource `navigation_bar_height`, which changes the boot image (see "Where the numbers are defined") and so has every application in `/data` compiled again.
- Rule 8's type sizes are converted from the literature; the smallest usable size on this machine has not been measured.
- The actual lightness of each grey level on the panel has not been measured from RAW photographs, so which levels the palette uses is not settled. A few small greys are therefore left as they are: progress bar tracks at grey 219 (level 13), the quick settings brightness track at grey 229, grey 224 on the apps and files pages, and AOSP's secondary text at grey 117 (`secondary_text_material_light`, `#8a000000` on white); each covers 0.4% to 5.7% of its region.
- A screen with an AppCompat theme is not reached by frameworks/base 0062: the support library carries a `primary_material_light` of its own (`frameworks/support/v7/appcompat/res/values/colors_material.xml:29`, still `material_grey_100`), and `Theme.AppCompat.Light` uses that one. Counting by region found no such screen among the system's own.
- The colour a third-party application gives its own notifications (`Notification.color`) is not changed: AOSP only adjusts it to at least 4.5:1 against white and does not make it black, where rule 2 asks for black. System notifications are black already (frameworks/base 0061); third-party notifications have not been measured on this machine.
- Settings and the all-applications list still scroll continuously; turning them into whole pages, as rule 6 asks, is not implemented.
- Recents shows only the six newest tasks; older ones cannot be seen and are removed only together, by "clear all".
